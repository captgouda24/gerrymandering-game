[[Gubernatorial elections]] were held in the [[United States]] on November 3, 2026. These elections took place in 36 states and three territories. The previous gubernatorial elections for this group of states took place in [[2022 United States gubernatorial elections|2022]], except in [[New Hampshire]] and [[Vermont]], where governors serve two-year terms and elected their governors in [[2024 United States gubernatorial elections|2024]].

Fifteen of the thirty-six states and two of the three United States territories with elections in this cycle had officially [[Term limits in the United States#Gubernatorial term limits|term-limited]] incumbents.{{Citation needed|date=November 2026}} The Democrats flipped at least five governorships, with two others ([[2026 Alaska gubernatorial election|Alaska]] and [[2026 Kansas gubernatorial election|Kansas]]) remaining undecided {{asof|2026|11|8|lc=y|df=us}}. As a result, this was the first time since [[2009 United States gubernatorial elections|2009]] that the Democrats won a majority of governorships.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/04/us/democrats-governors.html|title=Democrats will control most governorships, a generational shift|website=The New York Times|date=November 4, 2026}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.axios.com/2026/11/05/2026-midterms-democrats-state-legislatures-trifectas|title=Democrats poised to break GOP's 16-year grip on statehouses|website=Axios|date=November 5, 2026}}</ref>

== Partisan composition ==
Going into the election, there were 26 [[Republican Party (United States)|Republican]] governors and 24 [[Democratic Party (United States)|Democratic]] governors in the United States. This class of governors was made up of 18 Republicans and 18 Democrats. Republicans successfully defended two governorships in states that [[Kamala Harris]] won in 2024 (New Hampshire and Vermont). Democrats successfully defended five governorships in states that [[Donald Trump]] won in 2024 (Arizona, Kansas, Michigan, Pennsylvania, and Wisconsin).<ref>{{Cite web |date=2026-11-05 |title=U.S. Governor Midterm Elections 2026 Live Results - NBC News |url=https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/2026-elections/governor-results |access-date=2026-11-05 |website=www.nbcnews.com |language=en}}</ref>

== Predictions ==

Several sites and individuals published predictions of competitive seats. These predictions looked at factors such as the strength of the [[incumbent]] (if the incumbent was running for reelection) and the other candidates, and the state's partisan lean (reflected in part by the state's [[Cook Partisan Voting Index]] rating). The predictions assigned ratings to each seat, indicating the predicted advantage that a party had in winning that seat. Most election predictors used:
* "<u>tossup</u>": no advantage
* "<u>tilt</u>" (used by some predictors): advantage that is not quite as strong as "lean"
* "<u>lean</u>": slight advantage
* "<u>likely</u>": significant, but surmountable, advantage
* "<u>safe</u>" or "<u>solid</u>": near-certain chance of victory<!-- Colors and sorting:

{{See also|2026 Alabama lieutenant gubernatorial election}}
Governor [[Kay Ivey]], a Republican, was re-elected in [[2022 Alabama gubernatorial election|2022]] with 67.4% of the vote. She will be term-limited by the [[Constitution of Alabama]] in 2026 and cannot seek re-election to a third consecutive full term. Republican [[US Senator|U.S. Senator]] [[Tommy Tuberville]] entered the gubernatorial race in May 2025, joined by former candidate for [[Alabama's 6th congressional district]] in [[2024 United States House of Representatives elections in Alabama#District 6|2024]] Ken McFeeters<ref name="AL2026R">{{#invoke:cite|web|title=Qualified 2026 Republican Candidates|url=https://algop.org/qualified-2026-republican-candidates/|work=Alabama Republican Party|date=January 8, 2026}}</ref> and event center operations manager Will Santivasci.<ref name=ALcom>{{Cite web|url=https://www.al.com/politics/2026/02/alabama-2026-election-everyone-running-for-governor.html|title=Alabama 2026 election: Everyone running for governor|last=Gann|first=Heather|website=AL.com|date=February 5, 2026|access-date=February 8, 2026|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260206122742/https://www.al.com/politics/2026/02/alabama-2026-election-everyone-running-for-governor.html|archive-date=February 6, 2026|url-status=live}}</ref>

Democratic candidates include former U.S. Senator [[Doug Jones (politician)|Doug Jones]]; the [[2022 Alabama gubernatorial election|2022]] gubernatorial nominee Yolanda Flowers; [[Will Boyd]], a former city councilor of [[Greenville, Illinois]], and the nominee for Alabama Lieutenant Governor in [[2018 Alabama lieutenant gubernatorial election|2018]] and for U.S. Senate in [[2022 United States Senate election in Alabama|2022]]; pastor Ja'Mel Brown; candidate for governor in 2022 Chad Chig Martin;<ref name="AL2026D">{{#invoke:cite|web|title=2026 Candidates|url=https://aldemocrats.org/2026-qualified-candidates|work=Alabama Democrats |date=January 8, 2026}}</ref> and former state representative [[Nathan Mathis]].<ref name=ALcom/>

As Jones and Tuberville won their respective primaries, the gubernatorial election will be a rematch of the [[2020 United States Senate election in Alabama|2020 United States Senate election]], which saw Tuberville defeat Jones. Tuberville has faced several [[2026 Alabama gubernatorial election#Challenges to Tuberville's eligibility|challenges to his eligibility for the governorship]].

Governor [[Mike Dunleavy (politician)|Mike Dunleavy]], a Republican, was re-elected in [[2022 Alaska gubernatorial election|2022]] with 50.3% of the vote. He will be term-limited by the [[Constitution of Alaska]] in 2026 and cannot seek re-election to a third consecutive term.

Governor [[Katie Hobbs]], a Democrat, was elected in [[2022 Arizona gubernatorial election|2022]] with 50.3% of the vote. Starting with this election cycle, gubernatorial candidates were required to nominate a running mate for the newly established office of [[Lieutenant Governor of Arizona|Lieutenant Governor]]. Democratic Governor [[Gretchen Whitmer]] was re-elected in [[2022 Michigan gubernatorial election|2022]] with 54.5% of the vote. She is term-limited by the [[Constitution of Michigan|Michigan Constitution]] in 2026 and cannot seek re-election to a third term. 

Secretary of State [[Jocelyn Benson]] and [[Genessee County, Michigan|Genessee County]] Sheriff Chris Swanson announced campaigns for the Democratic nomination.<ref name=Benson>{{Cite web |last=Foster |first=Wells |title=Foster: REPORT: Secretary of State Jocelyn Benson 'accidentally' announces run for governor on social media |url=https://www.wilx.com/2025/01/22/report-secretary-state-jocelyn-benson-accidentally-announces-run-governor/ |access-date=January 21, 2025 |website=The Detroit News |language=en-US |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250122011527/https://www.wilx.com/2025/01/22/report-secretary-state-jocelyn-benson-accidentally-announces-run-governor/ |archive-date=January 22, 2025 |url-status=live}}</ref><ref name=Swanson>{{Cite web |last=Cappelletti |first=Joey |title=Chris Swanson, prominent sheriff and Democrat, announces run for Michigan governor |url=https://apnews.com/article/michigan-governor-sheriff-swanson-democrat-whitmer-3b77379b64a3b06f2d6043757598d35a |access-date=February 6, 2025 |website=[[The Associated Press]] |date=February 6, 2025 |language=en-US |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250207214311/https://apnews.com/article/michigan-governor-sheriff-swanson-democrat-whitmer-3b77379b64a3b06f2d6043757598d35a |archive-date=February 7, 2025 |url-status=live}}</ref> Benson easily defeated Swanson with 83.2% of the vote on August 4 and became the Democratic nominee.

Businessman, 2022 candidate for governor and [[2024 United States presidential election|2024 candidate for president]] [[Perry Johnson (businessman)|Perry Johnson]]; and [[United States House of Representatives|U.S. Representative]] for Michigan's 10th congressional district and nominee for U.S. Senate in [[2018 United States Senate election in Michigan|2018]] and [[2020 United States Senate election in Michigan|2020]] [[John James (Michigan politician)|John James]] announced bids for the Republican nomination.<ref name="JamesMI" >{{Cite web |last=Powers |first=Sara |date=April 7, 2025 |title=Rep. John James announces run for Michigan governor |url=https://www.clickondetroit.com/news/local/2025/04/07/rep-john-james-announces-run-for-michigan-governor/ |access-date=April 7, 2025 |website=WDIV |language=en |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250407213022/https://www.clickondetroit.com/news/local/2025/04/07/rep-john-james-announces-run-for-michigan-governor/ |archive-date=April 7, 2025 |url-status=live}}</ref> James defeated Johnson with 50.1% of the vote on August 4.

Detroit mayor [[Mike Duggan]], a former Democrat, announced his intention to run for governor as an independent in December 2024.<ref name="Duggan">{{cite web |title=Detroit Mayor Duggan, a longtime Democrat, will run for Michigan governor in 2026 as independent |first=Corey |last=Williams |work=[[Associated Press]] |date=December 4, 2024 |access-date=December 7, 2024 |url=https://apnews.com/article/duggan-detroit-michigan-governor-independent-democrat-5dba0e7b2d9304c2a00d716c9cd6ca05 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241207213101/https://apnews.com/article/duggan-detroit-michigan-governor-independent-democrat-5dba0e7b2d9304c2a00d716c9cd6ca05 |archive-date=December 7, 2024 |url-status=live}}</ref> He suspended his campaign in May 2026.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.freep.com/story/news/politics/elections/2026/05/21/mike-duggan-ends-michigan-governor-campaign-independent/90194635007/|title=Mike Duggan ends 2026 independent campaign for Michigan governor|first=Arpan|last=Lobo|website=Detroit Free Press|access-date=July 22, 2026|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260523035245/https://www.freep.com/story/news/politics/elections/2026/05/21/mike-duggan-ends-michigan-governor-campaign-independent/90194635007/|archive-date=May 23, 2026|url-status=live}}</ref>
